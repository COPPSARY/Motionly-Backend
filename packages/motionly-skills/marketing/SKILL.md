# Motionly Marketing & Video Sales Skill

## Purpose
Build a marketing intelligence and video-script generation skill for Motionly.
Motionly is an open-source motion graphics renderer/editor that treats animation like code. It allows users to create motion graphics programmatically and export them as videos.

The purpose of this skill is NOT to make generic "cool SaaS videos."
The purpose is to create marketing videos that:
- Understand the target customer.
- Identify a real problem or desire.
- Choose a deliberate marketing angle.
- Create a strong opening hook.
- Communicate a clear promise.
- Demonstrate the product as proof.
- Handle objections where appropriate.
- Create desire for the product.
- End with a clear action.
- Translate the sales strategy into strong visual storytelling and motion.

The final video should feel like an advertisement designed to produce action, not a slideshow decorated with animation.

## 1. Core Philosophy
Use the following principle:
**Marketing strategy comes BEFORE visual design.**

Do not begin by deciding:
- What animation looks cool?
- What transition should happen?
- What text should zoom in?
- What gradient should be used?
- What random UI card should appear?

## 2. Primary Reference Books
Treat these books as foundational sources of marketing principles.

### A. Scientific Advertising
**Author:** Claude C. Hopkins
**Year:** 1923
**Core concepts to extract:**
- Advertising is a form of salesmanship.
- Advertising should have a clear purpose.
- Focus on measurable response.
- Specific claims are stronger than vague claims.
- Demonstration can be more persuasive than unsupported claims.
- Know the customer.
- Test advertising rather than relying entirely on intuition.
- Avoid unnecessary cleverness.
- Make the proposition easy to understand.
- Give people a reason to act.

**Motionly application:**
Do not create an impressive animation merely because it looks impressive.
Ask: "What is this scene selling?"
Every important scene should have a marketing purpose.

### B. Cashvertising
**Author:** Drew Eric Whitman
**Core areas to research and extract:**
- Human buying motivations.
- Emotional triggers.
- Attention, Desire, Fear/problem avoidance, Social/status motivations, Convenience, Security, Curiosity.
- Benefit-oriented messaging.

**Motionly application:**
Do not describe Motionly only through features.
Instead translate: `FEATURE â†’ FUNCTION â†’ BENEFIT â†’ DESIRED OUTCOME`

*Example:*
Weak: "Motionly supports programmatic animation."
Better: "Build the animation directly from code."
Stronger: "Stop rebuilding the same motion graphics by hand. Define the animation once and make it repeatable."

The final version should connect the product capability to something the customer actually wants.

### C. Breakthrough Advertising
**Author:** Eugene M. Schwartz
**Research and extract:**
- Mass desire.
- Market sophistication.
- Customer awareness.
- Levels of awareness.
- Channeling existing desire.
- Differentiating the mechanism.
- Making the product relevant to an existing desire.
- Matching the message to the market's current state.

**Important principle:**
Do not assume every customer needs the same advertisement.
Before writing a script determine:
- Who is the audience?
- What do they already know?
- Do they know the problem?
- Do they know solutions exist?
- Do they know Motionly exists?
- Are they comparing alternatives?
- What do they already believe?
- What objections do they have?
Then select the message accordingly.

*Example:*
A founder who has never heard of Motionly: "Making product videos shouldn't require a motion designer every time."
A developer: "Your motion graphics can be version-controlled like the rest of your product."
Same product. Different awareness. Different advertisement.

### D. Great Leads
**Authors:** Michael Masterson and John Forde
**Research:**
- Types of leads: Problem leads, Promise leads, Story leads, Curiosity leads, Direct leads.
- How the opening determines whether the audience continues.

**Motionly application:**
The first seconds of a video are not an introduction to the company.
Do NOT default to: "Meet Motionly." or "Introducing Motionly."
Instead determine what the audience should care about immediately.

*Possible opening strategies:*
- PROBLEM: "You've already built the product. Now you need a video to explain it."
- CONTRAST: "Most motion graphics are built frame by frame. Motionly lets you build them like code."
- CURIOSITY: "What if your product demo could be generated from the same code that creates your animation?"

### E. The Adweek Copywriting Handbook
**Author:** Joseph Sugarman
**Research:** Headlines, Hooks, Flow, Psychological progression, Features vs benefits, Curiosity, Maintaining attention, Transitions between ideas, Calls to action.

**Motionly application:**
A video script should have a natural progression. Do not make every scene feel like an isolated slide (e.g. a feature slideshow).
Instead: `Problem â†’ tension â†’ realization â†’ solution â†’ demonstration â†’ payoff â†’ CTA`
Each scene should cause the next scene to make sense.

### F. Tested Advertising Methods
**Author:** John Caples
**Research:** Headlines, Appeals, Testing, Advertising structure, Specificity, Benefits, Attention, Measuring response.

**Motionly application:**
Generate multiple hooks and angles when useful.
Do not assume the first idea is the best idea.
For important campaigns generate: 3â€“5 hooks, 2â€“3 marketing angles, 2â€“3 CTA approaches.

### G. The Robert Collier Letter Book
**Author:** Robert Collier
**Research:** Entering the customer's existing conversation, Connecting the product to an existing desire, Making the message feel relevant, Focusing on what the customer wants rather than what the company wants to say.

**Motionly application:**
Do not start from: "What do we want to tell people about Motionly?"
Start from: "What is the customer already thinking about?"
The advertisement should join the customer's existing problem.

### H. Ogilvy on Advertising
**Author:** David Ogilvy
**Research:** Advertising strategy, Research, Headlines, Visual communication, Product demonstrations, Commercials, Brand communication, B2B advertising.

**Motionly application:**
Use visuals to communicate information rather than decorating the screen.
When showing Motionly:
DO: Show the actual product, workflow, transformation, output, before/after, mechanism, meaningful interaction.
DON'T: Replace the product with random abstract SVGs, show fake dashboards unrelated to Motionly, animate UI simply because animation is available.

### I. Influence
**Author:** Robert Cialdini
**Research:** Social proof, Authority, Scarcity, Reciprocity, Commitment and consistency, Liking, Unity, Ethical persuasion.

**Motionly application:**
When appropriate, incorporate legitimate proof (real users, projects, GitHub activity, etc.).
Never fabricate users, testimonials, revenue, or product capabilities.

## 3. Motionly-Specific Marketing Framework
Every generated video should first create a **Marketing Brief**.

**Marketing Brief:**
- TARGET AUDIENCE: Who exactly is watching?
- CUSTOMER SITUATION: What are they currently doing?
- PROBLEM: What is frustrating, expensive, slow, difficult, risky, or inefficient?
- DESIRE: What do they actually want?
- CURRENT ALTERNATIVE: What are they using instead?
- AWARENESS LEVEL: What does the audience already know?
- MARKET SOPHISTICATION: How familiar are they with existing solutions?
- CORE ANGLE: What single idea should the advertisement be built around?
- HOOK: Why should they continue watching?
- PROMISE: What outcome does Motionly offer?
- MECHANISM: Why/how does Motionly provide that outcome?
- PROOF: What can we actually demonstrate?
- OBJECTION: Why might they not believe or try it?
- CTA: What should they do next?

## 4. The "One Big Idea" Rule
A short advertisement should generally have one dominant marketing idea. Do not attempt to sell every feature.
BAD: Motionly is open source, code-based, fast, beautiful, has an editor, renderer, presets...
BETTER: "Motion graphics you can build like software." Then every scene supports that idea.

## 5. Product Demonstration Rule
When the advertisement reaches the product section, use the REAL Motionly UI whenever possible.
The product demonstration should answer: "What does this actually do?"
The viewer should see: Starting state, User action, Motionly response, Result, Final output.

## 7. Visual Storytelling Rules
Motion should communicate meaning. Every major animation should have a reason.
Use: Transformation, Spatial relationships, Camera movement, Scale, Timing, Direction, Continuity, UI interaction, Visual cause and effect.
Avoid: Random zooms, Random fades, Excessive text, Generic floating cards, Unrelated SVG illustrations.

### Newly Researched Addition: Building a StoryBrand
**Author:** Donald Miller
**Year:** 2017
**Core Principle:** The customer is the hero of the story, not your brand. Your brand is the guide who provides a plan to help them overcome a problem and achieve success.
**Marketing Problem It Solves:** Confusing, self-centered messaging that causes customers to tune out.
**Framework:** The SB7 Framework (Character, Problem, Guide, Plan, Call to Action, Avoid Failure, End in Success).
**When to Use It:** When constructing the core narrative flow of the video or when the current script feels too focused on Motionly's features rather than the user's outcome.
**When Not to Use It:** When creating a purely technical product demonstration for an audience that already deeply understands the problem and just wants to see the specific mechanism.
**Motionly Application:** Position Motionly as the "Guide" (the enabler), not the "Hero". The video should focus on what the user (the Hero) can achieve with Motionly.
**Example:**
*Hero:* A product marketer.
*Problem:* Needs a polished launch video but relies on slow, manual animation.
*Guide (Motionly):* Provides a programmatic way to build animations.
*Plan:* "Define the animation once and make it repeatable."
*Success:* Launch video ready on time without starting from scratch.
**Conflicts With Existing Principles:** None, it strongly reinforces the "Marketing strategy comes BEFORE visual design" and "Translate features into desired outcomes" principles.
