# Scene Blueprints

A blueprint is a product-agnostic shot *shape* — what happens across the scene's full
duration, paced so reveals land as beats rather than dumping everything at t=0. Pick the
shape whose description matches the scene's job, then build it with the moves from
`motion-rules` and the handoffs from `transition-techniques`. If nothing here fits,
compose freely — this is a menu, not a checklist.

## The shapes

**Kinetic type beats** — A flat, centered, bold-type shot where the motion *is* the
words changing: a fixed line swaps tokens in place by hard cut, or a statement builds
across beats onto a spring-pop payoff. Reach for it whenever the words carry the shot
and there's no set, surface, or click.

**Typewriter reveal** — A live caret types (and edits) a line as a human would, then
collapses it and pops a payoff, or holds it under a persistent mark while a sub-line
types into a final call to action. Use when "someone is typing this" should be the
engine.

**Spatial pan stations** — Pre-placed labeled stations on one oversized canvas,
traversed by a single virtual camera — repeated pans centering each station and
revealing a callout, landing on the last. Good for a milestone timeline, a connected
web of related points, or a two-shot bridge into a live demo.

**Camera journey** — The camera itself is the storyteller: a multi-leg motivated
journey (dive in → a beat fires → travel to the consequence → landing push) across one
continuous scene. Two sub-shapes: an action round-trip (a click fires, the camera
swoops to where the consequence renders), or a cursorless cinematic flight (motion
blur, depth of field, tilt-to-flatten). Use when the camera's travel itself tells the
cause-and-effect story.

**Zoom-out reveal** — Open tight on one full-bleed detail, let a micro-action play in
close-up, then one continuous decelerating zoom-out reveals the containing whole; the
frame locks and element-level payoff carries on. The structural inverse of a push-in.
Good for opening on a mystery detail that re-scopes into context, or landing a
scale/breadth payoff.

**Constellation hub** — Iconned nodes spring into a ring around a center, then resolve
on the core — a camera push-in, a held hub mark with orbiting satellites, or a click
that collapses the orbit and springs a demo out of it. Good for "it connects
everything" or "sits at the center of your stack."

**Grid/card assemble** — N items (tiles, cards, logos, list lines) self-assemble in a
staggered cascade into a grid or list and hold; an optional camera zoom-out reveals the
array inside a vaster whole. Good for enumerating breadth at once.

**Logo assembly** — A brand mark or wordmark comes to exist on screen: built from parts
(elements assemble/orbit, letters cascade, an outline draws on), spring-bloomed from
zero, or morphed out of the preceding phrase — resolving into a centered lockup,
optionally extended to a call to action. Good for a wordless brand sting or a
brand-outro beat.

**Cursor-led UI demo** — A visible cursor (see `cursor-lead`) drives a reconstructed UI
through clicks/hovers/drags so the screen changes state shot-to-shot, while the camera
chases each interaction or holds a locked stage while element swaps do the work. Good
for a first look at a surface, or a workflow demonstrated end-to-end onto an action
button.

**Device/surface showcase** — A device mockup or floating window held as hero while its
screens cycle through a real flow, presented by a camera ranging from a static hold to
a continuous 3D push. Good for showing a feature experienced inside its real interface.

**Prompt-submit-generate** — The "watch me ask, watch it answer" shot: a prompt/query
types into a real product input and the product answers — a streaming result, an action
log, diff cards, a chart, or a generated artifact. Keyboard drives, not a clicked-through
UI (that's the cursor-led UI demo) and not bare typed typography (that's typewriter
reveal).

**Agent progress theater** — Work performed as working-state theater: a single trigger
beat hands the frame to the process — loaders spin and status phrases swap while it
visibly works, then the receipt cascades in (a checklist whose rows arrive and check
off, or a thread building message-by-message to a confirmation). Good for dramatizing
multi-step work where the state mutation *is* the demo.

**Panel edit live-sync** — A bipartite stage — an inspector/editor panel bound to a
target surface — where a control is continuously manipulated (a value scrub, a
dropdown pick, a drag) and the coupled surface updates live in the same beat. Good when
the feature *is* live editing: "change this, watch it change."

**Data count-up** — Numbers and charts are the hero: a count-up ring/number, a trend
chart, a stat grid — traversed by a camera that pushes through or scrolls across them
to land on one hero metric. Good when the data carries the argument.

**Title card** — The calm breather/landing beat: one clean title or proof card revealed
with exactly one restrained move (a slide-up cross-fade, or a wipe-away-to-reveal), then
a still hold. Low motion is the payload, not a deficiency. Can also run as a chain of
2–3 near-still cards seamed by hard cuts.

**Comparison split** — Two paired items of equal weight enter from opposite sides with
mirrored 3D "book-open" tilts and hold side by side, then a badge spring-pops on each to
punctuate. Good for an A/B or "X plus Y together" — not more than two items, and not
sequential steps.

**Overwhelm/surround** — Recognizable elements assemble, density-marker icons scatter
in, then elements close in from all sides (surrounded, not zoomed into). Good for "you
are buried" pain points.

**Ticker takeover** — A typed lead-in plus an accent word cycling through options, then
a hero crashes in from off-screen and physically shoves the text aside — a collision,
not a fade — settling alone. Good when a "could be many things" build should be
violently replaced by "this is it."

**Fixed-anchor cycle** — One element stays pinned (a wordmark, a composer box) while
the adjacent region cycles through many discrete states: hard-cut label swaps, a
vertical carousel, per-word highlight stepping, or in-place theme morphs — resolving on
an emphasis beat into a completed lockup. Good for asserting breadth around one fixed
identity.

**Video-text pivot** — A video holds center and breathes, then slides aside to hand its
weight to a hero stat, then both clear and kinetic text types into the vacated center.
Good for "see the feature, see the impact" where the video must stay visible.

**CTA morph press** — A resting brand mark condenses at the same center into a
brighter call-to-action, then a cursor lands a click with feedback (see `cursor-lead`).
Good for a focused "click here" sign-off. The same shape widens to a Hook opener: a lone
widget on a flat field morphs in place, performs its payload, then vanishes to a typed
closing title.

## Picking guidance

1. Match the scene's job to a shape above.
2. Read its signature move and build it with `motion-rules` and, at scene boundaries,
   `transition-techniques`.
3. Adapt freely — keep the signature move, change the content and surface to fit the
   brief. If nothing fits, compose from `motion-rules` directly rather than forcing the
   wrong shape.
